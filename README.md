## Export Pattern Changes

The graph comparing the top 10 destinations for U.S. exports in 2015 and 2025 shows several important changes in U.S. trade patterns over time. Overall, U.S. export values increased during this period, especially with major trading partners. Canada and Mexico remained the two largest destinations for U.S. exports in both years, showing the continuing importance of North American trade relationships. One notable change is that Mexico surpassed Canada as the largest export destination by 2025. In addition, the gap between the top two export destinations and China became larger over time, suggesting that trade within North America grew more strongly compared with U.S.-China trade. This may reflect changes in supply chains, trade policy, and geopolitical tensions during the 2020s.

The graph also shows some changes within East Asia. Exports to Japan and Hong Kong became relatively less important by 2025, while Taiwan became a more important export destination. At the same time, several European countries, including the Netherlands and Switzerland, increased in importance. Overall, the results suggest that U.S. exports became more concentrated in traditional allied regions such as North America and Europe, while the relative importance of some East Asian destinations declined.
